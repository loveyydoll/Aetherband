# Aetherband — Messaging Guide

This guide keeps public descriptions consistent with the product direction and early-development status. Do not describe planned features as shipped.

## Positioning

**Aetherband is an open creative platform for making music.**

It is an experimental, free and open-source DAW being built for musicians, with its architecture and development visible to contributors. The long-term goal is a flexible workspace for recording, arrangement, sound design, and mixing whose core technology can evolve in the open.

## Core tagline

**Make sound your own. Keep the tools open.**

## Short descriptions

### GitHub repository description

An experimental open-source DAW built with C++ and JUCE, targeting Windows and macOS. Early development.

### One sentence

Aetherband is an open creative platform for musicians exploring a flexible, expressive approach to digital audio workstations.

### Short project introduction

Aetherband is an early-stage, free and open-source DAW moving toward a native C++/JUCE desktop application. We are building in the open toward an expressive workspace for recording, arranging, sound design, and mixing. The current C++ foundation is not yet a usable DAW; the roadmap describes the goal, not finished functionality.

### Social profile

An open creative platform for making music. Native C++/JUCE DAW in development. GPL-3.0-or-later goal. Built in the open.

## Claims policy

- Do not claim a production-ready DAW, complete workflow, or parity with commercial DAWs.
- Do not claim Windows/macOS audio support until the relevant platform and device tests pass.
- Do not claim FLAC/MP3/other format support from WAV testing alone.
- Do not describe Flutter or Rust as the target architecture; they are legacy references during migration.
- Do not promise the native project format, plugin hosting, or HypnoSynth port before implementation and verification.
- Describe GPL-3.0-or-later as the project license goal, and verify dependency/distribution terms before release.

## Tone

Creative, candid, curious, musician-centered, and technically honest. Talk about what the software helps people make, but distinguish aspiration from current capability.
